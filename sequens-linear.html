Great teaching, made reliable.

Schools are not short of committed teachers or ambitious leaders. What schools are increasingly short of is implementation coherence.

SEQUENS exists to provide implementation coherence architecture that enables schools to translate their values and strategic direction into observable, coachable and sustainable classroom practice - without overwhelming the humans inside the system.

A structured coaching system embedded in existing Monday planning time. A framework of observable classroom behaviours sequenced from simplest to most complex; coaching leads who observe fortnightly and give one precise adjustment; a monitoring layer that catches drift before it becomes reversion. None of the three is new. The architecture is in how they connect.

What the research is clear about.

SEQUENS is designed and engineered on this research. Each finding below is built into the architecture.

Who built this. SEQUENS is built by one person: a qualified teacher and senior leader who started out designing learning resources for children with SEND in mainstream schools, ran an alternative provision for young people out of education, taught primary through to leading teaching and learning internationally, and returned to the UK as an instructional coaching lead. It is built alongside a teaching timetable, which is why it is designed around the week a teacher actually has rather than the one a planning template assumes.

SEQUENS suggests. A human confirms. Every observation, every next move, every record is proposed and then agreed by the people involved. Nothing is generated, logged or acted on until somebody says so, and the reasoning is always shown so the decision is an informed one.

Coaching is the professional development that actually works.+

Across 60 causal studies, instructional coaching moved teaching practice by 0.49 standard deviations. That is a large effect in education research. The same meta-analysis found something else, and it is the reason SEQUENS exists: as coaching programmes scale up, their effects shrink to a fraction of what the small trials showed. Coaching works. Keeping it working across a whole staff is the unsolved problem.

SEQUENS architecture is coaching, not courses: a coach, a real classroom, a fortnightly cycle.
Kraft, Blazar & Hogan (2018), Review of Educational Research 88(4), 60 causal studies · EEF effective-PD reviews
Improvement is a practised skill, not delivered information.+

Skill grows through deliberate practice: small, specific actions, rehearsed with feedback, repeated until automatic. Rehearsing one precise move, many times, changes a great deal.

SEQUENS architecture is deliberate practice: one concrete move, rehearsed toward embedded. The embed judgement rests with the coach, not a counter.
Ericsson on deliberate practice · Deans for Impact
One thing at a time is a principle, not a limitation.+

Change embeds when it is singular and incremental; ask for many things at once and the load overwhelms. The strongest models work one bite-sized action at a time. The EEF's first mechanism is to manage cognitive load.

SEQUENS architecture is one move at a time: a teacher never sees the 367, only the next one. This page holds itself to the same rule.
Cognitive-load theory · EEF mechanisms · Bambrick-Santoyo
Trust is the mechanism, so coaching stays away from judgement.+

Coaching works only when a teacher can be honest about what is hard. Blur development into appraisal and teachers perform rather than reveal, and the coaching quietly stops working.

SEQUENS architecture is a wall: notes stay between teacher and coach, and leaders see cohort movement, never the conversation. Coaching is never appraisal.
Psychological-safety research · EEF; Sims et al.
We think in words and pictures, so teaching uses both.+

Working memory runs verbal and visual channels; pairing a clear explanation with a simple, matched image lets more land and stay than either alone. It runs right through the behaviours SEQUENS is built on.

SEQUENS architecture is dual coded: a picture beside every idea, through the domains and through this page.
Paivio · Mayer's multimedia learning · Caviglioli
Inclusion is a foundation, not a feature.+

Every one of the 367 behaviours carries its own SEND and EAL guidance, a note inside each move about the learners most easily left behind.

SEQUENS architecture is inclusive by design: reaching every child is the condition on all of it, not a feature beside it.
SEND & EAL guidance embedded across all 367 behaviours
Teaching that responds in the moment beats deciding in advance.+

Adapting to what a pupil actually does, during the lesson, achieves more than allocating tasks by group beforehand. A group decided in advance becomes a ceiling; a teacher reading the room does not.

Every SEQUENS move is defined by what you would see and what you would do about it, so a coaching conversation is about responding rather than about planning harder. Support and challenge live inside the lesson, never in a separate plan.
DfE ITT Core Content Framework, adaptive teaching · Black & Wiliam · EEF feedback guidance
Checking understanding beats assuming it.+

A question whose wrong answer tells you which misconception a pupil holds is worth more than any amount of reviewing at the end. Without one, a lesson is taught blind.

The moves include a mid-lesson checkpoint, and a coaching cycle can take it as its single focus. What is coached is whether the teacher finds out in time, not whether the lesson looked good.
Black & Wiliam, formative assessment · Wiliam, hinge-point questioning · Nuthall
Novices learn from worked examples and clear modelling.+

Learners with little prior knowledge gain far more from a worked example with the thinking made audible than from being left to work it out. Guidance early, independence later.

Modelling is one of the observable moves in the framework, sequenced early because it is the one that carries the most for the least experienced teachers and the least experienced pupils alike.
Sweller & Cooper, worked examples · Kirschner, Sweller & Clark · Rosenshine
Memory is strengthened by retrieval, not re-reading.+

Recalling knowledge changes it. Retrieval practice produces more durable learning than reviewing the same material again, and spacing those retrievals multiplies the effect.

Retrieval is an observable move in the framework, so it is coached as a practised habit rather than mentioned once in training and forgotten by half term.
Roediger & Karpicke · Dunlosky et al. · Cepeda et al., spacing

The architecture.

It is an implementation coherence architecture - not a new initiative, but the structure beneath your existing ones that helps them cohere, sustain, and improve over time.

Eight domains: four a teacher works on in the classroom, four that hold the system up. No teacher ever sees all of it; that is the point.

The cycle.

1Diagnose

An honest starting picture: self-review, and a first look-in.

2Select the step

One specific behaviour, where it will help most.

3Rehearse it

Practised before the lesson, not in front of thirty children.

4Teach it for real

The move goes into a live lesson.

5Feedback, briefly

A look-in on the protected window, then a short conversation. Length is the coach's call.

6Embed, then gate

Automatic first. Only then does the circle open to the next move.

Coaching is not appraisal.

A teacher's coaching conversations and notes stay between them and their coach. They are never fed into appraisal and never seen by leaders as individuals. It is what makes honesty possible, and a wellbeing safeguard a school can stand behind in any inspection.
