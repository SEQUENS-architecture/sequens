The same activity, three expectations

Nobody is doing a different afternoon. They are all sorting the same creatures out of the same pool. What changes is what you expect back.

Ivyage 5
Sort them into groups
Crabs here, shells there. Then say why they belong together, out loud.
What you are asking for
  • Groups that hold together
  • A reason said aloud, not written
  • One feature named: "it has a hard bit"
You have got it whenShe sorts them without you, and the reason is about the creature rather than about where she found it.
Otisage 8
Build a classification key
Yes-or-no questions that split the group. Then test it on a creature you did not use.
What you are asking for
  • Questions with only two answers
  • Each question splits the group properly
  • The test on a new creature actually works
You have got it whenThe key sorts a creature he had not thought about when he made it. That is the whole point of a key.
Nellage 12
Explain the adaptation
Why that shell, that claw, that colour, in that pool. Argue it from what you saw.
What you are asking for
  • A claim about a specific feature
  • Evidence from the pool, not from memory
  • Because, not then. The mechanism, not the sequence
You have got it whenShe says because and means it: "the limpet clamps down because the tide takes the water away."

You run one activity, not three.

That is the whole design. You are not managing three lessons on a beach. You are running one afternoon, and SEQUENS is holding three different expectations behind it so you do not have to.

And no child is told which one they are. Ivy is not on the easy task and Nell is not on the hard one. They are all rockpooling. The expectation lives with you.

Notice what it did not do. It did not claim the tides work was a curriculum objective, because it is not one. It said enrichment, and it will keep saying enrichment however useful the afternoon was.

And it told you Nell's argument-from-evidence sits a year above her age, rather than quietly filing it as normal. SEQUENS will not tell you that you covered something you did not.

Three children is where this actually matters. With one child you could hold the expectations in your head. With three, at different ages, on a beach, in the rain, you cannot. That is the job.

Gaps are normal. Every family has them. If Otis's key does not work on a new creature, that is not a failed afternoon. It is the most useful thing you can record, because the next session is built from it.

Nothing about your children is sent to an AI. SEQUENS asks a model for a rockpooling activity. It never hears their names, their ages, or a word of what you recorded.
