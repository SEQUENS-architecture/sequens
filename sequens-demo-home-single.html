MMaya · age 8

Why do shadows change through the day?

A hands-on science session about light and shadows, sparked by Maya noticing her shadow was longer in the evening.

About 40 minutes, or split over two sittingsYou'll need: a sunny window or torch, a small toy, chalk or paper
Curriculum link: this matches Year 3 science (light & shadows). Maya is 8, so this is right on track.

Start with what she noticed

5 minutes · together

Bring it back to Maya's own observation - that her shadow looked different in the evening. Ask her what she thinks is happening before you explain anything. Try asking: “When was your shadow really long - morning or evening? Where do you think the sun was?”

Show her how it works

10 minutes · you lead

Use a torch (or the sunny window) and a small toy. Shine the light from low down, then from high up, and watch the shadow change length together. Say something like: “Watch the shadow when I hold the torch down low - see how long it gets? Now high up… short. The lower the light, the longer the shadow.” Little one wriggly? Let Maya hold the torch and be the one to make the shadow grow and shrink. Doing beats watching.

Let her try & check she's got it

15 minutes · Maya does

Ask Maya to draw around the toy's shadow with the torch low, then again with it high - two shadows, same toy. Then the check: can she explain why they're different? The one question that matters: “If you wanted the longest shadow, where would you put the light?” If she says “low down” - she's understood it. No printer, no chalk? Trace the shadows onto any scrap paper, or just talk them through. It works either way.

Finish & make it stick

5 minutes · together

Round it off by linking back to the sun. The sun is low in the morning and evening (long shadows) and high at midday (short ones) - just like the torch. Last question: “So why was your shadow so long in the evening?” Let her tell you this time.

Afterwards - nothing you have to do

If you'd like to, snap a photo of Maya's shadow drawings for her portfolio, or just tick it off. Either way, SEQUENS quietly adds this to her coverage map.
