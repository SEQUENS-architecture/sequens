How does a bridge hold up so much weight?

A build-and-test session sparked by a trip over a big suspension bridge - one activity, two levels of thinking.

About 45 minutesYou'll need: paper, tape, a few coins or small weights, scissors

How this works with two ages: you run one shared activity so nobody's left out - but Otis and Nell each get their own goal and their own thinking. You're not teaching two lessons at once; you're pitching one, two ways.

Start together

5 min

Show them a picture of the bridge from your trip. Ask them both: what stops it collapsing under all those cars? Take every guess, no wrong answers yet.

Otis · Year 6 level

Focus his thinking on shape: which shapes are strong?“Have you noticed bridges have arches and triangles in them?”

Nell · Year 10 level

Push her toward forces by name: where is the bridge being pushed, and where pulled?“Where do you think it's under tension, and where under compression?”

The shared build

20 min · both together

Same task for both: using only paper and tape, build a bridge across a 20cm gap between two books that holds the most coins. This is the fun bit - let them build, fail, and rebuild.

Otis's goal

Make it hold as many coins as he can, and spot which shape worked best.He learns by doing - let him just try things.

Nell's goal

Test a folded (corrugated) beam vs a flat one, and explain why the fold is stronger.She's testing a hypothesis, not just building.

They'll want to compete - let them. Whose bridge holds more? The rivalry does the motivating for you.

Make sense of it

15 min · split

Now the thinking. Each explains what they found - at their own level. Have the younger one explain to the older, and the older one stretch further.

Otis explains

Which shape held the most weight, and why he thinks so.Check: can he say “arches/triangles are stronger”? That's the win.

Nell goes deeper

How the shape spreads the load - compression along the top, tension along the bottom - and where she's seen it in real bridges.Check: can she use “tension” and “compression” correctly?

Finish together

5 min

Back to the real bridge from your trip. Ask each of them to spot one thing in the photo they now understand - Otis a shape, Nell a force. Done.

Afterwards - two children, two records

One shared afternoon, but each child keeps their own coverage. SEQUENS logs it at the right level for each of them, so both maps stay honest.

Otis's map: Science & D&T → Structures & strong shapes (practised) Nell's map: Physics → Forces → Tension & compression in structures (practised)
