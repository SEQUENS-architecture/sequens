Beach package · for Otis

Rock-pool sorting

Writing a recount · about 40 minutes · logs to the coverage map

Who is doing this? One activity. Three expectations, held behind it. Set by the Viewing bar at the top.
The same activity, two expectations

Nobody is doing a different afternoon. They are all writing about the same beach. What changes is what you expect back, and no child is told which one is theirs.

You run one activity, not three. You are not managing three lessons at the kitchen table. You are running one afternoon, and SEQUENS holds the different expectations behind it so you do not have to.
First thing

Talk it over

Ask Otis about the best bit of the beach. What happened first? Just chat - you're warming up his memory.

Show them how

Sort two, out loud

Take two finds and put them in the same pile, saying why as you do it. Then take one that does not fit, and say why not.

Build it together

Sort the pile together

Put everything you collected into groups, out loud, before anyone writes anything down. Disagree with each other. That is the useful part.

Quick check

Right order?

Mix up three moments and see if Otis can put them back in order. If yes, he's ready to write.

Their turn

Write the diary

Otis writes his beach diary using the sheet. The "fastest find" challenge keeps his fast-blue-hero energy in it.

Last thing

Read it back

Read it together and celebrate one brilliant sentence. That's the bit he'll remember.

Year 8 · History · for Nell

Why did war break out in 1914?

Causes of the First World War · ~60 min · a full curriculum lesson, themed for Nell

National Curriculum link: KS3 History - "the First World War and the Second World War" (challenges for Britain, Europe and the wider world, 1901–present). Historical concept: cause & consequence. (2014 programmes of study - the current statutory curriculum.)
First thing

The spark

June 1914: one assassination in Sarajevo. Recall who Franz Ferdinand was - and why a single death could matter this much.

Show them how

Model the long-term causes - MAIN

Militarism, Alliances, Imperialism, Nationalism. Build them on the Spokes Frame as the pressures that were already building before 1914.

Build it together

Connect the spark to the system

Use "so that…" chains: the alliance system meant a local crisis pulled in the great powers, so that within weeks six empires were at war.

Quick check · hinge

Catch the misconception

"Which BEST explains why one assassination became a world war?" - A) it was simply a tragic murder · B) the alliance system dragged the great powers in · C) Germany alone wanted war · D) it was inevitable. Many choosing A or C → re-model the alliance web.

Their turn

Build an argument

Nell writes a paragraph prioritising the most significant cause and justifying it - colour-coded support, from sentence stems to a counter-argument challenge.

Last thing

Rank & justify

Exit task: rank MAIN + the trigger, and finish - "The most significant cause was… because…". A GCSE-style judgement in miniature.

◷ Frame · Spokes blank - model it

Nell fills the branches with you

📖 Nell's reading Y9 · interest-themed

Picture 1914 Europe as a strategy board where every power has locked into a web of treaties. Pull one thread - a shot in Sarajevo - and the whole board reacts: Austria moves, Russia counters, Germany commits, and within weeks the alliances drag six empires into a war almost no one chose.

Pitched to Nell's reading level and interest (strategy games). Private use; shared exports use neutral framing.

Our approach to Otis’s education

Otis is educated at home through a blend of planned, structured lessons and interest-led, real-world learning. We use the England National Curriculum as a guide for coverage and progression, while allowing Otis’s curiosity to shape how and when topics are explored. Learning takes place through focused sessions, family trips and projects, daily reading, and everyday life - chosen to suit his age, ability and the way he learns best.

What Otis has been learning

English. Otis writes for different purposes - most recently a beach recount using time-order language - and reads fluently at an early-fluent level. He is building stamina in extended writing.

Mathematics. Number and place value, measurement (length, mass, time), and an introduction to equivalent fractions, much of it applied practically through measuring, cooking and telling the time.

Science. Living things and their habitats (grouping and classification, explored at rock pools), the parts and life cycle of plants, and how we see light - hands-on and enquiry-led.

Foundation subjects. History, geography, art and PSHE covered broadly through projects and trips - local history, coasts and the water cycle, and personal safety.

Learning beyond the curriculum

This year Otis completed 12 cross-curricular real-world projects, including a full day’s study at the beach, a nature classification walk, baking (following a method and measuring accurately), and a local museum visit. These develop curiosity, independence and the ability to apply learning in context - strengths of a home education.

How we record Otis’s learning

We keep an ongoing portfolio: photographs of finished work, written pieces, and short voice recordings of Otis reading aloud and explaining his thinking. A representative sample is available on request.

Focus for the year ahead

  • Extend written pieces with more detail and a wider range of connectives.
  • Secure equivalent fractions and progress to comparing and ordering fractions.
  • Continue building scientific vocabulary through hands-on enquiry.
