One afternoon, five threads. That is the promise on the front of SEQUENS and this is what it looks like on paper. A sheet that only did the sorting would have thrown away the maths, the writing and the geography that were all standing in the same rock pool.

Every child does every section. There is no age on this sheet and no version of it. A five year old draws three shells, counts them, and tells you the pools fill up when the sea comes back. A twelve year old measures how far the tide moved in an hour, writes the recount with reasoning in it, and argues the adaptation. Same five questions. Their own answer to each.

Every question is open at both ends. "How many, how big, how far" works if you can count to five and it works if you can calculate a rate. That is what lets one sheet serve a whole family without labelling anybody.

And the coverage is honest. The geography is marked as enrichment rather than a set objective, because it is, and it stays marked that way however good the afternoon was.
